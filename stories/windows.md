Vira has two kinds of windows. Six small opening ports, and the two long fixed windows on the cabin sides. The ports took one summer. The fixed windows took me two years and four tries.

## Ports: a template and a mistake
February 2022 I made a jig to cut the port openings. The ports I bought have an angled spigot. That's good for drainage but a lot more work, because the cut through the plywood core has to follow the angle. My router bit is only 2.5 inches long, so the template is two layers. It lines up the cut in the outer skin and sets the angle.
@s136
In July I took the first window out to see how. Pull the trim, cut the side of the frame, fold it inward and out. Then template, route the hole. The old opening was 3–5 mm bigger than the new port, so not much grinding, mostly the slope for the spigots. I added a sloped plate inside for 2 more degrees, and measured the thickness for screws: #10, 1-1/4 inch.
@s217
Then the first port went in, the pilot. Short story: I bought self-draining ports by mistake instead of rain-draining ones. Not a big difference, except the Irwin 34 cabin sides are 17° from vertical, and self-draining ports allow 15° max. So I needed the inside wall closer to vertical. I calculated that 3 degrees more would do it and put a sloped shim inside under the plywood. Plus some reflective insulation to cut the heat through the cabin sides. The port went in on 3M 5200, as Bomar recommends.
I had planned through-bolts and even bought them. Then I changed my mind and used self-tappers like the original. With the tilted spigot, the bottom bolts came too close to the edge and the top ones came out under the outer frame. And a 1-inch #10 screw will break the plastic before it pulls out of epoxied plywood. If a storm could break the windows, I'd put covers on outside instead of trusting screws.

## Five more ports
With my son's help I took out the old, cracked, leaking plastic windows. Metal will come later, or much later. Cut the old ones out, removed the old inner plywood, and added the slope shims, CNC-cut so the angle is exact. Another 1/2-inch plywood strip below each window supports the interior plywood. The old sealant, looked like 5200, hadn't held to the plastic. I'll ask Bomar what prep they want.
@s232
Then I cut the inner plywood panels and epoxied them in, held with wood screws and temporary plates outside, ready for the other five ports.
@s236
August, a long day: route five new openings with the CNC template. At the thickest spot I had to cut through about 1/4 inch of outer glass, 3/4 inch of original plywood, about 1/2 inch of angled spacer and 1/4 inch of inner plywood, none of it square. Even the 2-inch bit couldn't reach. So I routed with both templates as deep as I could, took the add-on template off, and cut deeper from the top.
Lots of dust. The ports went in on 5200, maybe not the best choice, but the maker strictly recommends it. I soaked the openings with sealant first, then pushed the port in, so it's solid polyurethane between the port and every layer of plywood and glass.
Not many words, but a hell of a lot of work: cut, clean, mask, seal, and figure out how to clamp the outer trim rings on a curved surface. Sand bags help, as usual.

## Fixed windows, attempt one

## Attempt two, three, four...
Who designed it this way, I don't know. The frame gives only about 10 mm to bond the lens, and the surface is curved, so it's always pulling the ends of the lens out.
The first try was 1/4-inch acrylic cut to size, on the famous Sika 295 UV with activator. The sealant cost more than the acrylic. I followed every instruction.
In September 2022 I saw the starboard lens wasn't sticking. Probably I forgot to prime the acrylic. The other side was fine. Half a year later one window dripped: the Sika had let go of the plastic. I thought I'd messed up the prep or the timing, so I cleaned everything, scuffed the acrylic and did both sides again with Sika.
@s267:1-3
That one lasted a year, then dripped again, same way. My theory was UV going through the acrylic and killing the bond. So in May 2023 I tried the method everybody recommends: VHB tape and 3M 4000 UV. Cleaning off the old polyurethane was a lot of work. It bonds to fiberglass really well.
@s378:1-2
In August I filled the gap between the windows and the inner plywood. I'd put it off for a while: messy work that has to be done carefully so you don't damage the plywood face. I used epoxy with a bit of chopped glass for strength, 410 microlight so it sands easier, and 404 plus silica to get the right thickness. Filling an overhead gap is tricky. Next step: try to sand it without scratching the acrylic.
@s419
The VHB didn't survive half a year. I didn't check why, but I'm sure the small bonding area played a part.

## Screws, and a test first
I was tired of fighting adhesives. New plan, with a triple guarantee:
- Screw the lens down so the sealant never holds the load. A screw every 70 mm, 36 per window. Lots of holes, I know, but no stress on the bond.
- Use a different sealant on the lip: DOW 795. It holds glass on skyscrapers, so it should be good for us. DOW says it works with acrylic and Lexan, and lots of people online recommend it.
- Butyl tape around the outside.
Each window is 1,025 mm long and my CNC only does 740. So I cut each one in two positions, 400 mm apart, left side then right side. At each position: drill the holes, mill the slot around, then cut it out of the sheet.
@s17
@s18
This time I wanted one shot that lasts 5–10 years, so I did an adhesion test. I cut 15 pieces of FR4 circuit board, sanded to bare laminate at the contact spot, about one square inch, all sanded with 220.
For butyl tape I tested acrylic sanded at 220 and 600, Bed-It versus a white butyl tape from Amazon, cleaned with 50% IPA. All of them came off at about 6 lb. At 600 grit, neither tape stuck to the acrylic at all, nothing left on it. At 220 the Amazon tape held a little, not great, and Bed-It nothing.
For DOW 795 I tested glossy, 220 and 600, cleaned with IPA or TotalBoat dewaxer, and bare acrylic or epoxy-coated. Glossy acrylic held some, about 10 lb, and the silicone peeled off clean. Every other sample, whatever the prep, held until the silicone rubber itself tore in the middle, at 12–15 lb.

So, DOW 795 only, around the edge and in the gap. In December 2023 the new windows went in, screwed every 75 mm, the glass and gelcoat sanded with 220 and cleaned with 50/50 IPA.

## Finally dry
July 2025: no leaks for a year and a half. Now the inside could be finished. First I made two templates, with 20 mm and 30 mm corners. The 20 mm one fit best.
The screws worried me. They sit in fiberglass with silicone, and I don't trust that seal much, so I put rubber washers under them for a compression seal. Maybe I'm wrong, but washers are better than nothing. By the way, in later boats Irwin dropped the fixed windows and used regular ports.
@s676
Then new teak trim, each frame four pieces cut on my CNC in the garage. Only the gap between the glass and the trim is left to paint.
