Every few months I stopped and wrote down what was done. When you work weekend after weekend, it's easy to feel that nothing moves: you sand, you glass, you sand again, and the boat still looks like a construction site. Then you write the list, and suddenly you see how far you've come. Here are all my reports, in order. The lists are copied as I wrote them, item by item.

## Half a year: 211 hours

July 2022: 211 work hours so far. I went back to the list I made when I inspected the boat before buying her, and wrote next to each line where it stood:

- The liveaboard mess: cleaned out. Closed.
- Water damage: all the rotten wood is out of the interior. The plan is to rebuild it almost from scratch.
- Port lower front chainplate knee: rebuilt. Done.
- Scratched gelcoat: starboard side and bow patched and primed, port side planned.
- Leaking stanchions: all mounting blocks removed and rebuilt, stanchions to go back.
- Ports: new ones bought, the pilot one installed. Big fixed windows replaced.
- Deck: old cabin handles and the drip rail removed and holes patched, small voids this year, full repaint later.
- Furler: good, but the forestay's top Sta-Lok needs redoing, plus a 4-inch spacer plate at the bottom for the shorter forestay.
- Sails: off and washed. The main has lots of small holes, the genoa needs some care but is good, and a second genoa was found in a locker.
- Soft engine hatch: re-cored and re-glassed. Seal later.
- Ugly RV AC: works, but still ugly. A 12,000 BTU marine unit is bought and its shelf made.
- Steering: everything tightened, good for the lake. The port block plate needs redoing.
- Engine control: new aluminum plate, control remounted, gearshift cable replaced.
- Shrouds: wrong-size chainplates, and the mast was tilted 3–4 inches to port. New chainplates made, mast straightened.
- Fuel gauge glass replaced, showing 1/4 tank. Still want to look inside.
- Bulkheads: starboard was already wet again, so replaced and tabbed to the hull. Port tabbed on one side.
- Starboard front lower anchor: several layers of 1708 and 24 oz roving added, to sleep better.
- Companionway: slides and doors rebuilt, two boards made from teak with 1/4-inch acrylic windows. Looks cool!
- The strange anchor well drain: it turned out to be an inch of old PVC pipe going to the cockpit drain. Pointless, and almost destroyed. A new, deeper chain locker will replace it.

And here's what I found along the way, which was not on any list: a burned-out alternator (replaced with a 60 A one I had in the garage), the leaking hull-to-deck joint, leaking deck drains, the damaged forestay, a ripped-out welded pin on the front chainplate, a broken sump pump with a clogged hose, a quarter-full holding tank with rotten straps, a mast step sagged about 1/4 inch with a strange "piece of poop" under it, engine mounts on lag bolts with the glass let go of the wood, a broken toilet with sweating hoses, dead masthead lights, engine alarms not working because of a corroded connector, no air filter element, a leaking raw water pump, exposed wood around the shaft log, flaking transom paint, and a broken compass. Most of them were already fixed, or had parts on the way. Half a year in, and the boat had told me all her secrets. Or so I thought.

## Report #2: she sails

September 2022:

- Forestay repaired: I cut the damaged part and added 4-inch link plates between the chainplate and the turnbuckle. The furler has room for it. Temporary, until new rigging.
- All chainplates and spreader connectors cut and bent. They still need a couple of welds.
- Sails cleaned, genoa fixed, furler repaired, genoa and main back on.
- New ports installed. Interior plywood panels on the cabin sides.
- New VHF antenna and cable, new Windex, and a handheld VHF for now. Good enough for the lake.
- Marine air conditioner installed and working.
- Brand new wiring, the right wire and no shortcuts in material, gauge or routing. New battery charger, two new flooded group 27 batteries, new bow nav lights and LED bulbs, 18 W LED spreader lights. Inverter bought, not installed yet.
- Cockpit drains replaced, engine room ventilation redone.
- Holding tank, sticky gray water hoses and the broken toilet out, porta-potty in.

And YES, I had a day of sailing on the lake. She is great! Ten months of work, and finally the reason for all of it.

## Since October 2024

February 2026, the big one:

- Cabin sole replaced with rigid vinyl. New cover on the manual bilge pump. 3D-printed breaker guards. Cockpit compass mounted lower. Foot pump connected to the water system. Pedestal guard.
- Engine hauled out and tested: new injector tips, timing set to spec, compression and oil pressure checked. Converted to fresh water cooling. An engine monitor I built for temperatures, oil pressure, coolant level and so on. Engine beds rebuilt, new engine mounts.
- Cabin table with a gas lift. All new cabin cushions and cockpit cushions.
- New navigation computer in an IP68 box with my baseboard. 12 V outlets.
- 6-gallon water heater, shower controller and thermostatic valve, LPG valve controller and LPG leak alarm.
- A sail pack for the main.
- Trucked from Lake Travis to a boatyard in Dickinson, TX, with a way out to Galveston Bay and the Gulf.
- Bottom paint stripped, hundreds of small blisters and a few dozen medium ones fixed. Two deep delaminations at the waterline re-glassed. The smashed front of the keel ground out and rebuilt.
- 40-year-old bronze gate valves replaced with Marelon ball valve thru-hulls. Its own thru-hull for the HVAC, plumbed with no air lock. Cockpit drains now go straight down, hoses crossed so they can't backflood. A 1-square-foot lightning ground plate.
- Barrier coat, three coats of TotalBoat Spartan, topsides in Alexseal two-part polyurethane.
- Mast cleaned and coated with Nyalic, mast wiring replaced, radar bracket, new LED steaming light and masthead tricolor/anchor light.
- Raw water strainer, new cutless bearing, PSS dripless seal, prop and shaft cleaned and polished with Lanocote. Rudder skin repaired, lower rudder bearing rebuilt, strut tightened and re-glassed, rudder stuffing box repacked.
- Boat moved to Kemah Boardwalk Marina.

Of course, the list never ends. Still waiting for later: a safety strap so the engine and LPG lids stay with the boat. The centerboard crack (not critical, the ballast is lead pellets in epoxy), the sloppy pivot that knocks when the boat rolls, and the lift line pipe. The front chainplate with the ripped pin. A loud buzzer on the engine alarm lamps. Epoxying the wood joints between the bulkhead pieces.
