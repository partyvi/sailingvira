## Before the conversion

Before I even started thinking about the conversion, I checked the water passages in the engine head and block. No scale, no corrosion. Good sign.

I DID NOT use original Yanmar parts for the 2QM20F. They're VERY hard to find, so prohibitively expensive, and if something fails it's almost impossible to fix in reasonable time. The only exception is the heat exchanger, built by Mr. Cool Marine.

## Off-the-shelf parts

A 2QM20 heat exchanger from Mr. Cool. I had it modified for 1-inch fresh water connections: the top one points in a different direction, and the bottom one got a 90° angle.

![Heat exchanger drawing](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiokfiYNuKPoIfFMCxb2QrTlW_HzWdz9LQLr_h13y6orPYVHKIynuvF9nD_Zp7Zgo16TKwv5hbgJlqDNJICVXI4hKMA2KoV_QjWcH6m7vTxjbdEiWaBx5qTpZ4_sNplGZcd1nMFc3BpLmbVZ15mh46bL4IA0bkwfzY3C65AHAzRDlo6CSOHLu1Lb_6APoy8/s1600/Untitled.png)

A Yanmar 129001-42005 fresh water pump, plus a Yanmar 121450-42350 pulley.

![Yanmar fresh water pump, store photo](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgzZPZc6lBf-qQjrHwLNS-vGZ1JeIaNIV-RbKYtAD2UYmSJaETr2vSZsvgHJgHaAnuTXSFrVuy_igRxD6xALJuhO1d_esVcflseeaztduojBI0sHopgurwqwcGf70yvhAH4Ptlf6yok6by142f6ViqoZBIGUggwIFvyehns-5PNQY4vB74kIiD3X-iVGH2T/s1600/Untitled.png)

An inline 3-way thermostat, 709200406. It's used on the Defender ATV and is supposed to be 82–85 °C.

![Inline 3-way thermostat, store photo](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEh6shqyDTZqlB97kuUzcecbygLjKRJl4S8HMRJaDe_i9Pn6su-EKgsFnSaBYH-fhGk9gm9d_CJMQsZI8sm42FWB3P0XQiFcOSOnwZ1RBdSiYqUpTzHdaQzJL5K1hhn-2XKMtw2BuDRocytnrjdI8JHl3rgTiSWMDQ2XCN7o037u0zXDx5KL5CYxy_nRCjqd/s1600/Untitled.png)

Story page: [Engine - Fresh water cooling](https://irwin34.blogspot.com/p/engine-fresh-water-cooling-in-progress.html)

The engine overheat alarm switch replaced with one from a 2GM20F, Yanmar 127610-91350, 95 °C.

A new 1/2 x 40-inch V-belt (D&D PowerDrive AX38, rubber, 1/2 x 40 inch OC). 40 inches works, but 42–43 would likely be much easier to install. Now, to put a new belt on, I have to pry it on while turning the engine, or take the water pump pulley off and put it back.

BSPT 3/4 to 1-inch hose barb adapters instead of the original water ports, so the hoses become 1 inch and carry as much coolant as the pump can push. The original 5/8 hoses are too small. They can cause hot spots on the engine, and with them too much thermal stress on the block and head.

1-inch heater hoses with 90° elbows on the fresh water side.

## Custom parts

A flat front engine head cover from 12 mm steel. The original part was my template. The temperature alarm sensor hole went where there was room on the water pump plate.

![Flat 12 mm steel cover on the engine head](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjJ5Y9eyGHMTlQB5LLqLmWkK0L0vXe6-LzLnY3qWUmwq-IOWRYQrBpV9T_DZ3aP11Y2faypLYDAVTcHqksxwvK_46ddWciGdBNjhoU1wmHFlBquUQLJ3ye1zT8zXOLhgS4Pigc0sKRqx6MCP3tNTBOxBhtY5Ha9sdAjAkq9JgxuKzydI47zBWSIVsdtdmsO/s1600/IMG_0249.HEIC)

The water pump mounting plate, the pump's base, also 12 mm steel. I made it from the Lexan template I used to tune the pump position. The pump sits so its output port points at the block's water inlet. One of the studs is shared with the front head cover. I checked the V-belt path: it clears the pump body by about 10 mm, so I'll need to keep an eye on the belt tension.

**The engine runs.**

Video: [The engine runs on fresh water cooling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wEJMYpkyAtA)

**The heat exchanger.** During installation I found a problem: on the engine, the heat exchanger inlet had no access. So I mounted the heat exchanger separately from the engine: above the wet muffler, as close to the engine as possible, level, and high enough that air runs out by itself. A little longer hoses, but much better access to the heat exchanger. It took two hours to get it tight, with about 2 inches between it and the wet exhaust hose. Finding a place for the coolant expansion tank was a little struggle too; for now it's on the wall to the right.

**The manual starter, finished.** Installed and tested on the boat. A bearing block in the engine room wall, and instead of a whole handle, an adapter for a standard sailboat winch handle. That solved two problems at once: how to make a handy handle, and where to store it. The full story with the photos is in the Engine story: [A hand crank, again](../engine/#c9).

Video: [The manual starter in action](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E6v5dPIL5Ig)

**The long run.** So far, about 15 hours since the engine went to fresh water cooling. Some people said it's good, some said it would kill the engine instantly. So far the engine works. Zero issues. I've run it several times for an hour or so at cruising RPM, about 2,000. On February 13, 2026 we motored 5 hours nonstop at 2,000–2,200 RPM. The engine found its thermal balance at about 76 °C coolant out, and 70 °C after the heat exchanger, going back into the block. No sign of overheating. The old Yanmar is doing just fine with its new life.

A lot of learn, check, try, source, return (thanks, Amazon), weld, paint, try again. Then the engine ran with dual-loop cooling. On the test run the digital sensor showed 68 °C max after an hour running free. Without load I can't make enough heat to open the thermostat fully, so more testing on the boat. One "95 °C" overheat switch tripped during the test. In a pot on the stove it tripped at about 65 °C. The other didn't trip in oil up to 150 °C, so it's probably not an alarm switch at all. Cooking utensils make excellent test equipment.
