My son gave me a B&G Halo20+ radar, and it needed a home on the mast. Ready-made mounts cost $300–500 or more, so I designed my own: one piece of aluminum for $114. It's also in my original diary article: [Rigid radar mount for $115](https://diary.sailingvira.com/p/rigid-radar-mount-for-115.html).

## A radar from my son

In August 2025 I got a great present from my son: a radar, a B&G (Navico) Halo20+. I didn't need it on the lake, but since the boat was going to big water, it would be a very good add-on to my navigation setup. There was no point making a real mount before the transport, but I wanted to test it with my OpenCPN setup. So I designed a "cheap and dirty" bracket to tie it to the mast, cut from plywood on my CNC and glued.

Paying $300–500 or more for a piece of aluminum, even one holding a $2K radar, is insane, I think. And off-the-shelf mounts are "universal", with small hinges and small stainless pins: weak points, and a place for galvanic corrosion. So, my own design.

Design zero was plywood, VERY temporary. I needed a two-hour lifetime. The boat was still on the lake, but I wanted to test the radar with OpenPlotter and OpenCPN before West Marine's return window closed.

Then the real mount. My requirements:

- Take 10x the radar's weight both down and sideways without damage. Say 100 kg down plus 100 kg sideways. I think the radar would come apart before 200 kg got to the mount bolts.
- Made for my radar and my boat.
- As few parts as possible, ideally one, simple to build or order.
- Aluminum, like the mast. Minimum fasteners.
- Under $130.

My design is one piece of 3/16-inch (4.7 mm) 5052-H32 aluminum, as simple as possible. SendCutSend quoted $114, plus $15–20 of primer and paint I already had.

![3D model of the aluminum radar mount](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhUoxEPm3xcZpaYilxAlUI4AcV7EKxy95yPZ5yBi0jvTycr1CrlcD7zznfHg4_tgY2hka3iGgRgsaAAgnR9esYF06ZDojYBT7Ah1m1uXqxVkjYJw20f1iLvUBroiBpCs7b_idot9FL2FgTxRySr7qbMvgHonTcrmSySnxxgbPUORoTazsd6FQVjpLQgHq26/s1600/R50.png)

In simulation it survived 200 kg with less than 2 mm of deformation. That's only a 20% margin at 200 kg, but a load like that means 25 G or a direct hit. Normally I expect no more than 5 G, even in extreme working conditions, so about a 4x margin. Here are the flattened design and some 3D views.
