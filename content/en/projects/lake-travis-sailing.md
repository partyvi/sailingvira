All this work is for one thing: to go sailing. These are the days on Lake Travis, 2021–2025: the people who came along, docks that move, a lake that dries up and then comes back, and a 76 mph gust. The story goes on in [Day sailing Galveston Bay](../galveston-bay-sailing/).

## Girls together

December 2021, Day 4. Nothing was done on the boat that day. We just sailed Sea Sun, my Hunter 19, from West Beach Marina to Commanders Point Marina, to keep her next to the Irwin 34. By the way, the name was decided: the Irwin is Vira now. A bit over two hours, about 6–7 NM, at 3–5 knots, in a nice 5–20 knot wind, 10–13 °C, with rain at the start. Thanks, Eugene, for the help. Now the girls are together!

Then came the sailing day, with a big crew: me, my son, my father and Eugene. Lots of fun. Mostly we were learning this new boat. The wind kept changing speed, which was a bit annoying, but some tacks were really fun. I learned how to handle the big genoa and the relatively small main. She heels pretty easily to about 20 degrees, then holds it there. We tried beating with 100° tacks, and it worked.

There were some incidents. We had an "intentional" man overboard drill: dropped the sails and motored back to pick up the "MOB". And water came in through a broken PVC pipe that ran from the "chain locker" to the cockpit drain thru-hull. The open end of the broken pipe went under water when the boat heeled to port, so we got 5–6 gallons in the bilge. Very minor. I clamped a rubber hose between the thru-hull and the PVC pipe, so it can't happen again.

## Docks that move

In late August Vira went from the repair dock to a pocket slip, much easier for going sailing. That's the good news, and probably all of it. The slip is only about a foot wider than her beam, the corners have no protection, and it's the fourth dock from the lake, so every wake rocks the boat. I spent money on dock wheels. To fit 12-inch wheels I had to set the mounts flush with the dock; otherwise the boat couldn't get in and out. I started early, to get a couple of hours in before the sun got high and the real Texas hell began.

## Swapping slips

After the marina turned the whole dock and Vira's transom ended up too close to the shore, the owner agreed that I could swap slips with the boat across the walkway. We did it one evening after work. A good workout. The part I was nervous about went VERY easily: turning Vira in a tiny space, backing all the way out through the narrow water and around to the office dock. I worried about prop walk, but it wasn't as bad as people said. Then we had to move the neighbor's boat into Vira's old spot by "walking" her along the docks with boat hooks. About 600 feet, and she didn't want to go straight. A couple of times we drifted too far and had to "fishtail" the rudder to get back. About an hour of fun, and both boats were in their new slips.
